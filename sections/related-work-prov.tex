\section{Related Work: Provenance Graphs in Systems Security}
\label{sec:related-prov}

\paragraph{The provenance bet.} Systems security made the structural bet long before agents existed: whole-system provenance graphs record causal dependencies among processes, files, sockets, and syscalls, and whole-graph detectors reason over them to find stealthy intrusions invisible in any single event~\cite{zipperle2022provenancebased}. The paradigm supports streaming deployment~\cite{goyal2024orchid} and efficient learned detectors over provenance subgraphs~\cite{xu2024procsage}. Crucially, the community also learned its limits the hard way: mimicry attacks that interleave benign activity around a small malicious backbone---preserving degrees, paths, and other structural statistics---defeat provenance-graph detectors, motivating detection over graph \emph{semantics} and continuous evaluation under adaptive adversaries~\cite{goyal2023sometimes}.

\paragraph{Transferring the bet to the agentic Web.} Agent tool-call traces are, in a precise sense, application-layer provenance: an LLM process reads observations, transforms them into decisions, and emits tool invocations whose results become subsequent observations. But the transfer is not mechanical, and three differences define our design space. First, agent graphs are \emph{typed semantically} rather than by syscall class: a node is a payment call or a file write, and its meaning binds to the task, so schema design is a first-class contribution rather than a fixed OS abstraction. Second, the unit of analysis is one user session---graphs of tens of nodes---rather than weeks of host activity, so detectors must operate on small graphs where whole-graph classification, not node scoring, is the natural inference. Third, the adversary does not merely inject structure; it generates the trajectory through a compromised controller, so anomaly families must be produced by \emph{replaying real attacks}, which is what our data engine does. IPIGuard is the closest bridge: it represents tool dependencies as a graph, but as a fixed defense-time plan constraint rather than a learned detector over observed provenance~\cite{an2025ipiguard}. We supply the missing half---corpus-level, learned, whole-graph auditing---and we import the mimicry lesson directly into our evaluation protocol instead of rediscovering it after deployment.
