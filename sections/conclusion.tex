\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced TraceGraph-Bench, the first benchmark that audits LLM agent tool-call trajectories as heterogeneous provenance graphs with graph-level anomaly detection. The benchmark couples three anomaly families---injection hijack, malicious use, and over-privilege---under one typed graph schema, and its protocol builds the controls that recent audits of safety evaluation demand into the spine of the design: leave-one-attack-family-out generalization, cross-environment transfer, mimicry robustness in the lineage of provenance-graph attacks, a disclosure ablation separating structure from text, and audit-economics accounting. A systematized detector matrix, from rules to graph foundation models to a supervised heterogeneous-graph reference detector, provides the first map of this new problem, showing where structure alone suffices, where text is necessary, and where adaptive splicing breaks the structural bet. We release the compiler, traces, graphs, and harness to make trajectory-level auditing reproducible on consumer hardware.

\section*{Limitations}
Three limitations bound our claims. First, replayed trajectories reflect the model families and suites we could host locally; behaviors of frontier agents may differ, which is why the benchmark publishes the compiler as a protocol rather than a fixed dataset. Second, our mimicry variants are scripted transformations rather than a learned adversary; they bound robustness below a fully adaptive attacker, and we deliberately omit a feedback-loop attack arm from this version. Third, A3 over-privilege is defined against explicit grants; real deployments often lack such specifications, and extending the family to inferred grants is future work.

\section*{Ethics}
This work studies defensive auditing of agent behavior and creates no new attack capability beyond what is already public in the source benchmarks we replay; injection payloads and harmful-task specifications are inherited from AgentDojo, AgentDrift, and AgentHarm under their respective licenses and are used solely to construct labeled audit data. All trajectories are synthetic or replayed in sandboxed environments with simulated tools; no real user data, accounts, or systems are involved. We will release data and code under licenses that permit defensive research while preserving the upstream benchmark licenses.
